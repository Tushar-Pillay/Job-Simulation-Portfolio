## 📌 Overview

This project was completed as part of the **Mastercard Advisors & Consulting Services Virtual Experience Program** hosted on Forage.

The simulation focused on helping **GreenTrail Outdoors**, a fictional sustainable outdoor recreational equipment company, improve the effectiveness of its in-store promotional strategy.

The project simulated a real-world consulting engagement involving promotional data analysis, statistical analysis, Test & Learn methodology, control-group design, strategic recommendation development, and data-driven storytelling.

The objective was to identify the most promising promotional approach, design a controlled experiment, and develop a sustainable strategy that could improve customer engagement, sales, and return on investment (ROI).

---

# 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze GreenTrail Outdoors' historical promotion performance
- Compare different promotional strategies
- Identify patterns in sales and customer visits
- Evaluate promotion performance across store types
- Identify additional data required for stronger decision-making
- Develop an appropriate test and control strategy
- Determine an appropriate test duration
- Recommend a sustainability-aligned promotional strategy
- Translate analytical findings into a client-facing business story
- Develop actionable recommendations focused on ROI

## Task 2 – Test & Learn Strategy

### Key Activities

- Identified limitations in the historical dataset
- Developed a test and control framework
- Recommended comparable control stores
- Considered store characteristics, historical sales, traffic, geography, and product mix
- Recommended a 4–6 week testing period
- Defined KPIs for evaluating promotional effectiveness

### Recommended Testing Approach

**Test Group:**
Stores receiving the new sustainable promotion.

**Control Group:**
Comparable stores that do not receive the new promotion during the testing period.

### Measurement Framework

**Incremental Impact = Change in Test Stores − Change in Control Stores**

This approach helps distinguish the true impact of the promotion from normal changes in sales.

# 📊 Key Business Insights

### 1. Special Events showed the strongest historical performance

Special Events generated:

- **29.89% average visit uplift**
- **$39,375 average post-promotion sales**

This makes Special Events the most promising format for further testing.

### 2. Urban stores showed stronger sales performance

Average post-promotion sales:

- **Urban: $40,461.54**
- **Suburban: $35,916.67**

However, store type alone should not determine treatment assignment. Comparable stores should be matched using multiple characteristics.

### 3. Historical data does not prove causality

All 25 stores in the historical dataset received promotions.

Therefore, the historical analysis shows **observed performance**, but it cannot prove that the promotion itself caused the sales increase.

A controlled Test & Learn experiment is required to measure incremental impact.

### 4. Experience-led promotions provide a sustainability opportunity

Special Events provide an opportunity to move beyond traditional discounting and create customer experiences centered around sustainable products and practices.

# 🧪 Test & Learn Framework

The proposed experiment follows a simple:

## TEST → MEASURE → SCALE

### TEST

Launch the Sustainable Outdoor Experience Event in selected test stores.

### MEASURE

Compare performance against comparable control stores over a **4–6 week** period.

### SCALE

If the promotion produces positive incremental sales, profit, ROI, and sustainable-product engagement, expand the strategy to additional stores.

# 💡 Consulting Framework Used

The project followed a structured consulting approach:

**1. Define the Problem**

Understand GreenTrail's promotional effectiveness challenge.

**2. Analyze the Data**

Evaluate sales and customer visit performance across promotion types and stores.

**3. Identify the Insight**

Identify Special Events as the strongest historical promotion format.

**4. Develop the Strategy**

Combine Special Events with GreenTrail's sustainability positioning.

**5. Design the Experiment**

Create comparable test and control groups.

**6. Measure Impact**

Evaluate incremental sales, profit, ROI, and customer engagement.

**7. Recommend & Scale**

Scale the strategy only if the controlled test demonstrates positive results.

---

# 🚀 Final Recommendation

GreenTrail Outdoors should test a **Sustainable Outdoor Experience Event** based on the historically strong performance of Special Events.

The strategy should combine sustainable product experiences, repair and reuse initiatives, recycling or trade-in activities, and customer education.

Rather than immediately rolling out the strategy, GreenTrail should conduct a **4–6 week controlled Test & Learn experiment** using comparable test and control stores.

Success should be evaluated using **incremental sales, incremental profit, ROI, customer engagement, and sustainable-product performance**.

If the test demonstrates positive incremental ROI and supports GreenTrail's sustainability objectives, the strategy can be scaled across additional stores.
